Radar Privacy Policy

Last updated: Sep 30, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Radar (the "App"), an Android app published on Google Play by Fis GA Studio ("we", "us" or "our"), handles information when you use it. Radar gathers the news flashes that Israeli news outlets publish on their websites into one feed, with alerts. It has no user accounts, and we don't run servers of our own. Radar is free and shows one banner ad from Google AdMob.

The short version

  • No sign-up, and no analytics or tracking tools of our own.
  • Your settings and the flashes Radar found stay on your device. We don't receive them.
  • Radar shows one banner ad from Google AdMob, which collects device data such as your advertising ID and IP address to show and measure ads.
  • Radar connects from your device to the news outlets' websites and to Google services, which receive your IP address like any website you visit.
  • We don't sell your personal information for money.

1. Information stored on your device

Radar saves on your device the sources you follow, your alert and display settings, the news flashes it found (kept for up to four days) and a short technical log for troubleshooting. We don't receive any of this.

If Android backup is turned on for your Google account, Android may include Radar's settings and data in your device backup. That backup is stored by Google under your account.

Settings > Status report shows a technical report on the screen. It leaves your device only if you copy it and choose to send it to someone.

2. Connections Radar makes

To find new flashes, Radar connects directly from your device to the public websites and feeds of the news outlets you follow (for example N12, which publishes its reporters' chat through Google Firebase, Kan, Channel 13, Channel 14, i24NEWS, ynet, Walla, Maariv, Israel Hayom, Haaretz, Calcalist and others). It also loads the pictures that come with the flashes from the outlets' own servers. Radar shows each outlet as a colored badge with its name and doesn't load the outlets' logos (versions up to 1.8 loaded them from Google Play's image servers and Google's website icon service).

Like any website you visit, these services receive your IP address and standard technical details of the request, such as the device type in the browser identification text. They process them under their own privacy policies, for example the Google Privacy Policy and Privacy and security in Firebase. Radar doesn't add any identifier of you or your device to these requests.

When you open a flash's link, it opens in your browser or in the outlet's app. When you share a flash, Android's share menu opens and you decide where to send it.

3. Notifications and background activity

Radar creates its notifications on your device. It doesn't use a push server, and no one else learns which flashes you received. To notice new flashes when the screen is off, Radar keeps a live monitor running in the background, shown as an ongoing notification, and checks the sources on a schedule. You can pause it, change its speed or turn off any source in Radar's settings.

4. Advertising (Google AdMob)

Radar is free and shows one banner ad from Google AdMob at the bottom of the flash feed. It has no full-screen or video ads.

Ads are requested only after Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform) allows it where you live. To show, measure and protect ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK included in Radar automatically collects the following information and sends it to Google:

  • Device and account identifiers, such as the Android advertising ID and app set ID.
  • IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of your device.
  • Interactions with Radar and with ads, such as app launches and taps.
  • Diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage.

Google uses this information for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention and, where permitted, to personalize ads. Google processes it under its own policies. To learn more, see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services, the Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure and the Google Privacy Policy.

5. Your choices about ads

  • Consent: where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), Radar shows Google's consent message before your data is used for personalized ads. You can review or change your choice at any time in Radar under Settings > Privacy choices, which appears where it applies.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your Android settings (usually under Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads).
  • Google ad settings: you can manage how Google personalizes ads in My Ad Center.

If you opt out of personalized ads, you'll still see an ad, but it will be less tailored to you.

6. Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to download flashes, and to adapt the checks to Wi-Fi or mobile data.
  • Notifications: to alert you about new flashes. You choose the sources.
  • Foreground service, start at boot, exact alarms and wake lock: to keep checking on time when the screen is off and after a restart.
  • Battery optimization exemption: optional. Radar asks for it only when you choose to, so that Android doesn't stop the monitor.
  • Advertising ID: lets the Google Mobile Ads SDK read the Android advertising ID to show and measure ads (see section 4).

Radar doesn't ask for your location, contacts, photos, files, camera, microphone or access to other apps' notifications.

7. Sharing and transfers

We don't sell your personal information for money, and we don't receive the information that the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects. Information is shared only with Google (AdMob), to show and measure ads as described above, and with public authorities when the law requires it. Radar has no analytics or tracking tools of our own and no user accounts.

Some privacy laws, such as those of certain U.S. states, treat the use of advertising identifiers for targeted ads as "sharing" or a "sale" of personal information. You can opt out using the choices in section 5. Google may process information in countries other than yours, including the United States, using its own safeguards for international transfers.

8. Children

Radar is a news app intended for adults aged 18 and over. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

9. Security

Radar's data is kept in its private storage on your device, which Android isolates from other apps. Radar uses encrypted connections (HTTPS) to every source that offers them, and data sent by the Google Mobile Ads SDK is encrypted in transit. These measures reduce risks but can't guarantee absolute security.

10. Retention and deletion

Flashes are deleted from your device automatically after about four days. You can delete all of Radar's data at any time in Android's settings (Apps > Radar > Storage > Clear data) or by uninstalling Radar. Advertising data is kept by Google according to Google's retention policies.

If you email us, Gmail processes your email address, message and any attachments you choose to send, and we use them only to reply. Support messages are deleted from our mailbox, including the trash, within 90 days after your request is resolved. To ask for access to or deletion of support information we hold, email fisgastudioapps@gmail.com.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete your personal information, to object to or restrict certain uses, and to complain to your local data protection authority. Radar keeps your information on your device, so you can view and delete it there. For information held by Google, use Google's privacy tools.

12. This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages and doesn't use cookies or analytics. GitHub may log technical information, such as IP addresses, to run and secure the service. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

13. Changes to this policy

We'll update this policy when Radar's data practices change and change the "Last updated" date above.
